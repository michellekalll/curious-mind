## Suggested Short-Answer Points

13. Rule-based automation follows specified rules; ML learns
    patterns from data. A product may combine both.
14. Supervised learning uses labeled examples; unsupervised
    learning looks for patterns without provided labels.
15. Example: practice questions. Risk: incorrect explanations
    or overdependence.
16. Transparency makes relevant system information understandable.
    Accountability means people or organizations remain
    responsible for outcomes.
17. Check accuracy, relevance, completeness, bias, dates,
    and source reliability.

    Verify claims with reliable independent sources.

## Answer Explanations

1. **B** — AI is a field concerned with systems performing tasks associated with capabilities such as prediction, perception, language, or decision support.
2. **B** — Machine learning uses data or experience to identify patterns and produce predictions or decisions; it does not guarantee correctness.
3. **A** — The examples have labels (spam or not spam), so this is supervised learning.
4. **B** — Regression predicts a numerical value such as travel time.
5. **B** — Generative AI produces new content, such as text, images, audio, or code.
6. **B** — A hallucination is output that presents false, fabricated, or unsupported information as if it were reliable.
7. **C** — Responsible academic use includes following course rules and checking claims rather than copying unverified output.
8. **A** — Biased data, design, or deployment can produce unfair or unequal outcomes.
9. **C** — Avoid entering sensitive information into tools that are not approved for that information.
10. **B** — AI-generated references can be inaccurate, fabricated, or unrelated, so each source must be checked.
11. **A** — Overfitting occurs when a model fits training data too closely and generalizes poorly to new examples.
12. **C** — It specifies the audience, topic, length, and number of examples, making the request more actionable.

## Suggested short-answer marking guide

13. A strong answer explains that rule-based automation follows explicitly specified rules, while AI systems may infer patterns from data or use learned models. Answers should note that the distinction depends on the system; not every AI system learns, and AI systems can also include rules.

14. A strong answer states that supervised learning trains on labelled examples, while unsupervised learning looks for patterns or structure in data without target labels. One accurate example of each should be included.

15. A strong answer gives a plausible educational use (for example, practice-question generation or feedback) and a relevant risk (for example, inaccurate explanations, privacy exposure, or bias).

16. A strong answer distinguishes transparency (making relevant system use, limitations, or reasoning/evidence understandable) from accountability (identifying who is responsible for decisions, oversight, and remedy).

17. Any three suitable methods may receive credit: verify claims against reliable independent sources; open and check cited references; compare with course materials or expert guidance; check calculations/data; look for unsupported certainty or missing context.

18. A strong answer describes a concrete prompt improvement (such as adding context, audience, constraints, or desired format) and a separate verification method (such as checking claims against reliable sources).
